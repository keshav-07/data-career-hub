Senior interviewers rarely want "the right tool". They want to hear you **name a trade-off, pick a side for this scenario, and say what you give up.** Use this structure:

> "We could do A or B. A gives us X but costs Y. Because the requirement here is Z, I would choose A, and I would mitigate Y by W."

## The trade-offs that come up most

| Trade-off | Lean one way when | Lean the other way when |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| **Batch vs streaming** | Freshness of hours is acceptable; simplicity and cost matter | Decisions depend on data within seconds or minutes |
| **ETL vs ELT** | Data must be masked before storage; target cannot compute | Warehouse compute scales; you need to reprocess from raw |
| **Normalised vs dimensional** | Write-heavy operational data | Read-heavy analytics with simple queries |
| **Full refresh vs incremental** | Small tables; simplicity | Large tables; cost and runtime matter |
| **Exactly-once vs at-least-once + idempotency** | Within a system that supports transactions end to end | Across systems (the usual case) |
| **Partition by date vs by key** | Most queries filter by time | Most queries look up by key (then cluster instead) |
| **Managed service vs self-hosted** | Small team, speed matters | Strict control, special requirements, very large scale |
| **Strong vs eventual consistency** | Financial correctness, deduplication | Dashboards that tolerate seconds of delay |
| **Precompute vs compute on read** | Repeated heavy queries, strict latency | Ad-hoc, rarely used queries |
| **Wide denormalised tables vs star schema** | One consumer, simple access | Many consumers, shared dimensions |

## A worked example

**Question:** "Design the pipeline behind a daily sales dashboard for 2,000 stores. Some stores upload late."

Making the trade-offs explicit at each step:

1. **Batch or streaming?** "The dashboard is read in the morning, so freshness of a few hours is enough. I'd use a daily batch with an optional intra-day refresh. Streaming would cost more to run and operate for no decision that needs it; if store managers later want live figures, I'd add streaming ingestion into the raw layer and keep the modelling in batch."
2. **ETL or ELT?** "Sales data has no personal fields beyond loyalty ids, which I'd pseudonymise on ingestion; everything else lands raw and is modelled in SQL, so rule changes can be replayed from raw."
3. **Full refresh or incremental?** "Facts are append-mostly and large, so incremental by business date, overwriting each date's partition. Dimensions are small, so a full rebuild is simpler."
4. **Late stores.** "Partition by sales date, rebuild the last three days on every run, and show a 'stores reported' count on the dashboard so users know when a day is complete. That trades a little extra compute for correctness and transparency."
5. **Exactly-once?** "Not needed as a transport guarantee: partition overwrite makes reruns idempotent, which gives the same result."
6. **What would change my mind?** "If volumes grew a hundredfold I'd revisit partitioning and clustering by store; if finance needed figures frozen at a cut-off, I'd add a closing process instead of rebuilding three days."

Each step names the options, chooses for **this** requirement, states the cost, and says when to revisit. That pattern is what interviewers mean by "senior".

## How to sound senior

1. **Tie every choice to a requirement** stated by the interviewer, or one you clarified.
2. **Quantify roughly**: "50 GB a day is fine for a single daily batch."
3. **Name the cost** of your choice and how you would mitigate it.
4. **Say when you would revisit**: "If the business later needs 5-minute freshness, I would move ingestion to streaming but keep modelling in batch."

## Common mistakes

1. Picking the newest technology without a requirement.
2. Listing pros and cons without deciding.
3. Absolute statements ("always use streaming").
4. Forgetting operations: who runs it at 3 am, and what it costs per month.
5. Not asking clarifying questions, so the trade-off is decided against an imagined requirement.
